# Portfolio Project 2: PetalMart Demand Forecast

Statistics for Data Science · after Module 09 · about 6 hours

## Business use case

PetalMart's supply team must order stock for the festive season now. It needs a 12-week forecast of weekly orders for each of its 10 categories, with a range to plan for, before Diwali on 8 November 2026. Diagnose the series, build three forecasting methods, test them honestly on the past, and write a 1-page planning note.

## Data source

`petalmart_demand` is **Coral Petal synthetic data**, generated by [`../datasets/generate_petalmart_demand.py`](../datasets/generate_petalmart_demand.py) with seed 2026. No real customer data is used.

```bash
cd statistics-for-data-science
python datasets/generate_petalmart_demand.py --seed 2026
```

Orders run from Monday 4 September 2023 to Sunday 30 August 2026: 156 full weeks. Something changed in one region partway through; finding it is part of the job.

## Ground rules

- **Weekly series.** Sum orders over regions for each category, in weeks ending Sunday (`pd.Grouper(key="date", freq="W-SUN")`). The calendar regressors are the number of Diwali-week days and sale days in each week.
- **Seasonal strength.** STL on the log of each weekly series, `period=52, robust=True`; strength = max(0, 1 − Var(remainder) / Var(seasonal + remainder)).
- **The break.** For each region, its share of weekly orders; compare the mean share over the 13 weeks after each week with the 13 weeks up to it, and report the region and week with the largest jump.
- **Backtest.** Rolling origin: train on the first 104, 108, …, 144 weeks (11 origins), forecast the next 12 weeks each time. MASE scales each origin's MAE by the mean absolute seasonal-naive error (lag 52) on that origin's training weeks. Coverage = share of actual weeks inside the 80% interval.
- **Seasonal naive.** Each week repeats the same week a year earlier; its 80% interval is ± 1.2816 standard deviations of the training weeks' seasonal-naive errors.
- **The forecast.** Weeks ending 6 September to 22 November 2026, with the method that has the lowest mean backtest MASE for each category.

## Tasks and deliverables

1. Diagnostics: plots, STL seasonal strength, the structural break (region, week, share before and after) and residual checks (Module 09).
2. Three methods per category: seasonal naive; exponential smoothing (ETS with a damped trend and a yearly season, on logs); SARIMAX with the Diwali-week and sale-day regressors (and Fourier terms for the yearly shape), with 80% intervals (Module 09).
3. The honest backtest (above): MAE, MASE and 80% coverage for each method and category, and the best method per category (Module 09).
4. A forecast table: 12 weeks × 10 categories with 80% intervals, and each category's 12-week total next to the same weeks last year.
5. A 1-page planning note for the supply team: what to order for, the range to plan for, which categories carry the most risk, and why (the festival moved; the East warehouse).
